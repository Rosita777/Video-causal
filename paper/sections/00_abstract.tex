Concept erasure in text-to-video generation targets specified semantic
content, yet a physical event can remain legible after its source weakens
through a residual splash, fracture, deformation, release, or trace.  We
formulate \emph{causal-role erasure}: suppress an entity in a registered source
role and reduce its dependent footprint while preserving the same noun in
registered noncausal uses.  We introduce \methodname, which distills toward
dynamic distributional source-free targets under a preservation update.
Relative to its Matched Control, \methodshort{} changes only the identity in the
factual source slot while holding the target and remaining training recipe
fixed.  Our registered main study compares three Wan streams---Original,
Matched, and \methodshort{}---with a separate five-stream CogVideoX benchmark
across seven physical mechanisms.  Generic Paraphrase and Bystander Token
controls on Water and Fracture probe surface-form and noun-frequency
explanations.  Full-video evaluation separates source and footprint efficacy
from receiver, quality, and specificity guardrails, and stratifies efficacy by
held-out-source and implicit-footprint cases.
