\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Removing an object from a generated video is not equivalent to removing its
participation in a physical event.  A ball can fade while water still
splashes; an impactor can vanish while fresh fragments remain; a load can
disappear while a surface stays deformed.  These residues are temporally
downstream evidence of the event that the edit was meant to suppress.  Recent
effect-aware video removal exposes this gap when editing an observed clip
\citep{motamed2026void,ekin2026beyondmasks}.
We study its model-level counterpart in open-ended text-to-video (T2V)
generation with text-only inference.  We operationalize the source-dependent
evidence following a registered interaction as its \emph{causal footprint}.

The noun alone is also the wrong intervention unit.  The same noun can denote
a causal source in one prompt and a bystander or otherwise legitimate
noncausal object in another.  Unconditional suppression may under-erase the
causal event by leaving its footprint, or over-erase the noncausal scene by
deleting every use of the noun.  Prior erasure methods typically specify a
semantic concept or category as the target
\citep{gandikota2023esd,xu2025videoeraser}.  Our contract instead suppresses a
registered source role and its dependent footprint while preserving
registered noncausal uses.  We call this task \emph{causal-role erasure} and
summarize its requirements as \emph{source--footprint--specificity}.

Three difficulties follow: footprints can be implicit, repeated source
identities invite noun memorization, and a prompt supplies no frame-aligned
no-source realization.  We therefore use a \emph{distributional source-free
target}: an independently generated dynamic video without the registered
source, trigger, or dependent footprint, but with the receiver and scene
preserved.

\methodname\ (\methodshort) trains a separate adapter for each mechanism.
It varies factual source-slot identity against a fixed source-free target and
alternates counterfactual distillation with generic preservation updates
(\S\ref{sec:method}). Figure~\ref{fig:task-method-overview} shows the shared
target and the two separate training arms; their only treatment difference
is the source identity.

We evaluate seven equal-status mechanisms and eight streams across Wan and
CogVideoX.  A same-backbone Matched Control isolates source-slot replacement;
two-mechanism lexical controls and held-out-source, implicit-footprint, and
specificity tests probe simpler explanations.  Full-video judgments separate
source and footprint efficacy from preservation.  This design licenses at
most a behavioral conclusion: it can test role-conditioned generation, not
discover or certify a latent causal model.\par

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We formulate model-level causal-role erasure for open-ended T2V around
        a source--footprint--specificity contract and gated full-video metrics.
  \item We introduce \methodname, which varies source identity while fixing a
        dynamic source-free target, and a single-factor Matched Control.
  \item We construct a registered seven-mechanism main study and a
        two-mechanism identification study with generalization, specificity,
        preservation, and failure analyses.
\end{enumerate}
